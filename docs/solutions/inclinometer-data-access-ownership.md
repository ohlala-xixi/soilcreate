For a slope, excavation, tunnel, or dam project, a [Shape Array (SAA)](/products/deformation-monitoring/flexible-inclinometer) or [in-place inclinometer (IPI)](/products/deformation-monitoring/in-place-inclinometer) is more than installed hardware. It is part of a long-term monitoring record. Engineers use that record to compare current movement with the baseline, review trends, and make decisions when conditions change.

That raises a question worth asking **before installation**: who controls the complete path from the sensor to the historical data?

## Inclinometer data access: a risk that can emerge after installation

Geotechnical instruments are often purchased through distributors, resellers, or project intermediaries. These arrangements can work well when the manufacturer, sales channel, and end user have clear agreements about device access and data ownership.

Problems can arise when device activation or platform access depends on an upstream provider's authorization. If a commercial or regional agreement later changes, the instrument may remain in the ground while the project team loses access through its expected platform.

Restoring service could require a new account, a different gateway, a platform migration, or additional site work. Historical readings may still exist, but the immediate concern is whether engineers can retrieve them and connect them to the ongoing monitoring record.

For a buried instrument, an interruption can affect trend analysis precisely when the project team needs reliable continuity.

## Five questions to ask a supplier

Before purchasing an SAA or IPI system, ask:

1. Who develops and supports the sensor, data acquisition unit, communications system, and monitoring platform?
2. Who can activate or suspend device and platform access, and under what conditions?
3. Can the end user export the full historical record, including raw data?
4. What happens to access if a distributor relationship or regional authorization changes?
5. How will new readings remain connected to the original baseline if the system needs to migrate?

These answers should be documented before equipment is installed, especially where replacing it would be costly or disruptive.

## Why working with the manufacturer matters

SoilCreate develops the core parts of its monitoring chain in-house, from sensing and data acquisition to communications and the monitoring platform. This gives customers a direct route to the team responsible for integrating and supporting the system.

For a long-term monitoring project, that means clearer responsibility when a technical issue affects data delivery. It also makes it easier to discuss access rights, historical data export, and a continuity plan with the people who build the system.

In-house development does not replace a written agreement on data ownership, access rights, data retention, and support.

A monitoring system will still need maintenance and support over its lifetime. What matters is knowing, from the start, who is responsible for keeping its data accessible.

**A buried instrument should provide a durable engineering record. Data access belongs in the product specification from day one.**
