Deep excavation failures are often documented after the event through surface cracks, settlement, emergency response, or post-incident investigation.

What is much more difficult to obtain is a continuous record of how the underground deformation actually developed before and during the incident.

This is what makes the Huajiachi Station case particularly valuable.

Public reports described leakage at a diaphragm wall joint, emergency response measures, and subsequent surface effects around the excavation of Huajiachi Station on Hangzhou Metro Line 15.

However, SoilCreate also retained **first-hand IPI monitoring data collected directly from the project site during the deformation process**.

These records were not reconstructed from later observations or compiled from secondary reports. They came from continuous in-place inclinometer measurements taken as the excavation response developed.

The data captured several important stages:

- Initial deformation after excavation unloading
- A sudden increase in inward retaining wall movement following the leakage event
- Development of a distinct deep deformation zone at approximately **31 m depth**
- Cumulative displacement approaching **70 mm**
- A clear relationship between deep underground deformation and the later surface response

<p class="seo-generator-spacer">&nbsp;</p>

This makes the Huajiachi Station incident more than a conventional project case study.

It represents a rare real-world deformation dataset that allows engineers to examine not only **how much movement occurred**, but also **when the deformation changed, where it developed, and how the risk evolved underground**.

## Project Background

Deep excavations for metro stations significantly change the stress conditions of the surrounding soil.

As excavation progresses, soil is removed from inside the retaining structure, earth pressure is redistributed, and the diaphragm wall and support system begin to deform.

In dense urban environments, this deformation may also affect roads, utilities, nearby buildings, and groundwater conditions.

For this reason, an effective **deep excavation monitoring** program normally combines surface measurements with underground **retaining wall monitoring** and **diaphragm wall monitoring**.

At Huajiachi Station, the IPI system continuously measured lateral deformation at different depths.

Unlike surface settlement monitoring, which mainly shows what is happening near ground level, **in-place inclinometer monitoring** helps engineers understand where underground deformation is developing.

## What Did the IPI Monitoring Data Reveal?

The engineering value of the Huajiachi Station case comes from the sequence of deformation recorded by the monitoring system.

The data did not simply show one final displacement value. It showed how the excavation response changed through several stages.

### Initial Deformation After Excavation Unloading

The first stage was the initial deformation associated with **excavation unloading**.

As soil was removed, the original stress balance changed and the retaining structure responded to new earth pressure and support conditions.

This initial **deformation profile** provides an important reference for later measurements.

Engineers can compare later profiles with the baseline to determine whether the retaining wall remains relatively stable or whether the deformation mechanism is beginning to change.

### Sudden Increase in Retaining Wall Movement

Following the reported **diaphragm wall leakage**, the monitoring data showed a noticeable increase in inward **retaining wall movement**.

This change is important because **displacement rate** can sometimes provide an earlier indication of abnormal behavior than cumulative displacement alone.

A wall that gradually accumulates movement over several weeks may represent a different condition from a wall that begins moving rapidly within a short period.

The accumulated displacement may initially be similar, but the risk development can be very different.

## A Deep Deformation Zone Developed at Approximately 31 m

One of the most important findings was the development of a distinct **deep deformation zone** at approximately **31 m depth**.

This was a critical monitoring signal because it showed that the deformation was not limited to the upper retaining wall or to the ground surface.

The movement was developing deep underground.

An **in-place inclinometer for deep excavation** measures lateral displacement at multiple depths and produces a displacement-versus-depth profile.

This allows engineers to determine whether deformation is concentrated:

- Near the top of the retaining wall
- Around the excavation level
- Near the excavation bottom
- Below the excavation
- Around a deeper movement zone or potential **sliding surface**

In this case, the approximately 31 m **deformation depth** helped identify where the underground movement was becoming concentrated.

<p class="seo-generator-spacer">&nbsp;</p>

### Why Deformation Depth Matters

The maximum displacement value alone cannot explain the deformation mechanism.

For example, two monitoring points may report similar maximum movement but have very different deformation profiles.

One may show relatively shallow movement, while the other may show a concentrated deep movement zone.

Understanding the **deformation depth** therefore helps engineers interpret the possible movement mechanism and determine whether additional investigation is required.

## Accumulated Displacement Approached 70 mm

As the event progressed, the recorded **cumulative displacement** approached **70 mm**.

This was clearly an important monitoring value.

However, 70 mm alone does not explain the full engineering process.

A single maximum displacement value answers:

**How much did it move?**

Continuous **excavation deformation monitoring** can answer several additional questions:

- When did the movement begin to accelerate?
- At what depth was the deformation concentrated?
- Was the deformation profile changing?
- Was the movement extending deeper?
- How did the underground deformation relate to the later surface response?

These questions help engineers understand how risk is evolving instead of simply recording the final result.

### Displacement Rate

A rapid increase in **displacement rate** can indicate that site conditions have changed.

This may occur after groundwater leakage, changes in excavation stage, support modification, or other unexpected events.

### Monitoring Threshold and Warning Threshold

Each project should establish a **monitoring threshold** and, where required, a higher-level **warning threshold** based on design requirements, applicable standards, site conditions, and the project monitoring plan.

However, thresholds should not be interpreted in isolation.

Rapid acceleration, a newly developed deep deformation zone, or correlated changes in several monitoring parameters may require engineering review even before a predefined displacement limit is reached.

### Surface Settlement and Ground Deformation

**Surface settlement** and other forms of **ground deformation** remain important parts of deep excavation monitoring.

However, surface measurements should be interpreted together with underground lateral displacement.

In some situations, deep deformation may develop before the surface response becomes obvious.

## Deep Excavation Monitoring with IPI

**Deep excavation monitoring with IPI** provides more than periodic displacement measurements.

An **IPI monitoring system** keeps multiple sensors installed inside the **inclinometer casing**, allowing deformation data to be collected automatically at different depths.

A typical **MEMS inclinometer** measures changes in inclination. These measurements are then used to calculate lateral displacement and build a continuous deformation profile.

This makes IPI particularly useful when site conditions may change quickly.

Typical advantages include:

- Automatic data collection
- Continuous lateral displacement profiles
- Higher-frequency monitoring
- Faster identification of deformation changes
- Reduced dependence on repeated manual site measurements
- Easier integration with automated monitoring and warning systems

For projects where deformation may develop rapidly, a **retaining wall displacement monitoring system** based on IPI can provide a more complete record of the deformation process.

## Why IPI Monitoring Matters for Deep Excavations

Traditional manual inclinometers remain widely used in geotechnical engineering.

They can provide reliable lateral displacement profiles, but the measurement frequency depends on site visits.

When deformation changes rapidly between two manual measurement intervals, part of the development process may not be captured in detail.

An automated **IPI monitoring system** reduces this gap by collecting data continuously or at predefined intervals.

This makes it useful for:

- Deep excavations
- Retaining wall monitoring
- Diaphragm wall monitoring
- Metro station construction
- Underground structures
- Projects near buildings and utilities
- Sites requiring automated deformation warning

The choice between manual inclinometers and IPI systems depends on project requirements, but continuous monitoring can provide additional value where rapid deformation is possible.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How to Monitor Deep Excavation Deformation?

To **monitor deep excavation deformation**, engineers typically combine underground lateral displacement measurements with surface settlement, groundwater monitoring, structural response, and other project-specific instruments.

For continuous underground monitoring, an IPI system can provide displacement profiles at multiple depths and track how those profiles change over time.

### How to Detect Deep Excavation Movement?

To **detect deep excavation movement**, engineers should review cumulative displacement, displacement rate, deformation depth, profile changes, and related surface response.

A sudden increase in movement rate or the development of a new deep deformation zone can be particularly important.

### What Is a Deep Excavation Deformation Warning?

A **deep excavation deformation warning** is generally triggered when monitoring data reaches project-defined criteria or shows abnormal behavior requiring engineering assessment.

Warning criteria may include cumulative displacement, displacement rate, deformation profile changes, groundwater response, or correlated movement across several monitoring systems.

### What Is a Retaining Wall Displacement Monitoring System?

A **retaining wall displacement monitoring system** measures lateral movement of a retaining structure during excavation.

Depending on the project, this may include manual inclinometers, IPI systems, total stations, or other deformation sensors.

### Why Is Deformation Depth Important?

Deformation depth shows where underground movement is concentrated.

This helps engineers distinguish between shallow wall movement and deeper deformation that may be associated with a potential movement or sliding zone.
